# Core Battle Box: Runner matchup guides

## 04 — Nebula Talent Management: Fast Advance

**Default plan:** take agendas from HQ and R&D before the Corp can score them from hand. When the identity is on its Gemilang Arena side, prioritize a reasonably cheap successful HQ/R&D run to flip it back. Finish turns untagged where practical: tags here threaten your money, resources and already-stolen agendas. Build only the rig needed to keep central accesses efficient.

This guide targets the 49-card list pictured in the [published Nebula deck image](https://cardgamer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Nebula.png) from Card Gamer’s Core Battle Box article, inspected on 9 October 2026. The linked live NetrunnerDB page could not be fetched. Advice is specific to this snapshot, not to every Nebula build. Numerical preferences are proposed bot defaults; exact click/credit examples state their assumptions.

### Deck snapshot

| Type | Published list |
|---|---|
| Identity | Nebula Talent Management: Making Stars / Gemilang Arena: Burning Bright |
| Agendas — 10 | 3 Embedded Reporting; 3 Next Big Thing; 3 Superconducting Hub; 1 Tomorrow’s Headline |
| Assets — 6 | 3 Regolith Mining License; 3 Spin Doctor |
| Operations — 17 | 1 Bigger Picture; 3 Government Subsidy; 3 Hedge Fund; 1 IP Enforcement; 3 Nanomanagement; 3 Petty Cash; 3 Predictive Planogram |
| Upgrades — 2 | 2 Mahkota Langit Grid |
| ICE — 14 | 3 Ping; 1 Flyswatter; 3 Funhouse; 3 N-Pot; 1 Whitespace; 3 Doomscroll |

There is no Measured Response, tag-gated damage operation, or access ambush. Do not assign this deck PT Untaian’s damage reserve or Zwicky’s Response budget. Damage comes from Doomscroll during runs. Tag punishment is still serious: IP Enforcement can remove points from your score area, and Bigger Picture can transfer a large part of your credit pool to the Corp.

### The key cards

| Threat | What it does to your plan | Default response |
|---|---|---|
| **Gemilang Arena, the back side** | Refunds a click for the first operation each turn; flips back on a successful HQ/R&D run. | Make an affordable productive central run before giving it another Corp turn. |
| **Nanomanagement ×3** | Cost 4, gives two clicks; net extra click from a normal play, with an additional refund if it is Gemilang’s first operation. | Flipping the identity does not eliminate all fast advance. Track available copies, money and scores. |
| **Petty Cash ×3** | Can be replayed from Archives before an action has finished, gaining its own extra click when played outside HQ. | An Archives copy plus Gemilang is a visible cheap scoring enabler. A run on Archives does not itself remove the operation or flip the identity. |
| **IP Enforcement ×1** | Pays X credits and removes X Runner tags to install a stolen X-point agenda; adds an advancement if tags remain. Trash cost 5. | Clear tags that enable repossession, especially when you hold a high-value agenda. |
| **Bigger Picture ×1** | While tagged, it can add a tag, or remove tags to make you lose up to 5 credits per tag and gain the actual amount lost. | Usually pay 2 and a click to clear a tag instead of risking this swing. Do not regard one tag as harmless. |
| **Doomscroll ×3** | Tag, then one net damage, then two more net damage if you have at least two tags. | Count tags from earlier ICE before choosing which subs to break. |
| **Next Big Thing ×3** | A 5/3 worth three points; its score or steal supplies a counter the Corp can spend with a click for draw/hand filtering, even from your score area. | Take the points, but anticipate that the Corp can improve its hand afterward. |
| **Mahkota Langit Grid ×2** | Rez 2, trash 2; two recurring rez credits at its server; adds 2 to asset trash costs, persisting for the run when trashed. | Count server-local rez money, and do not expect trashing it to lower the asset cost later in the same run. |

### Opening hand and setup

**NE01 — Prefer economy plus early central access; efficient multiaccess is a strong bonus.** A cheap breaker, useful run event or tutor can turn an opening into agenda denial. Eight of fourteen ICE are code gates, so a deployable decoder gains value; Ping still requires a barrier solution and Doomscroll may require a killer or calculated tanking.

Keep economy plus reliable draw even without a starting breaker if the hand develops quickly. Mulligan an expensive remote-focused plan that cannot interact with centrals for several turns. A complete rig is not a prerequisite for the first HQ/R&D run.

**NE02 — Play economy before optional hardware when it funds central pressure this turn.** Aim to pay for a useful access and any expected tag removal. Do not install a second expensive tool merely to improve a future perfect turn while a currently open R&D is feeding agendas into the Corp’s hand.

**NE03 — Favor access density over remote-building tools.** The Corp’s agendas frequently pass straight from HQ into its score area. Multiaccess on HQ or R&D can reach several of them before they vanish; repeated low-value remote checks do not. This is a priority adjustment within the actual Runner deck, not an instruction to assume a specific multiaccess card is available.

### Identity denial: when a central run is worth extra

**NE04 — When Gemilang is showing, prefer a useful HQ/R&D run costing roughly 2–4 credits over an optional setup action.** Include expected tag-removal expense in the evaluation. The run can both find agendas and remove the next turn’s operation-click refund. Raise the willingness to pay when Archives Petty Cash visibly enables a score or the Corp is near victory. Lower it when the Corp lacks funds/cards to exploit the refund and your run would cripple the next real contest.

This 2–4 range is a starting preference for routine denial, not a maximum price for stopping a win. An eight-credit single-access run is usually too expensive merely to flip the identity once unless its tempo effect is decisive.

**NE05 — A successful run is what flips it, not an access or steal.** You do not need to find an agenda. A successful HQ/R&D run with an access-replacement effect still meets the identity condition. Merely breaching HQ outside a successful run does not. A run on Archives or a remote does not flip it, however profitable that run is otherwise.

**NE06 — Do not pay repeatedly for a flip already achieved.** Once it is on the front side, another HQ/R&D run that turn supplies access/reward value but no second identity-denial bonus. Choose between a second access, removing an economy asset, clearing a tag, or developing the next turn on their actual merits.

**NE07 — Flipping it buys a different scoring budget, not an absolute lock.** The front side does not refund operation clicks during the action phase. If it plays an operation, it gains a credit and flips at action-phase end. That late flip does not retroactively grant an action click for scoring this turn. Nanomanagement can nevertheless produce sufficient clicks even on the front side.

### Concrete fast-advance thresholds

An agenda installed from HQ normally needs one installation action plus its advancement requirement in advancement actions. Scoring itself does not cost a click. These examples assume an empty legal remote, no other costs/modifiers, and the shown operations available. “Starting credits” is the minimum for the stated immediate sequence, without first taking other income actions.

| Starting identity and line | Actions available after the operations | Starting credits for the shown score | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Front: Nanomanagement | 4 | 7 | Install and advance a three-advancement agenda three times. |
| Back: Petty Cash from Archives as first action | 4 | 3 | Petty nets 2 credits; install and three advances leave 2 credits. |
| Back: Petty Cash from HQ alone | 3 | No amount fixes the action shortage | Its identity refund cancels playing it, but there is no outside-HQ extra click. Cannot alone install and advance three times. |
| Front: Petty Cash from Archives alone | 3 | No amount fixes the action shortage | Its own click refund is insufficient without Gemilang or another click source. |
| Back: Nanomanagement as first operation | 5 | 7 | Install and advance three times, with one action left; extra income/advancement can use it. |
| Back: two Nanomanagements | 6 | 13 | Install and advance Next Big Thing five times. |
| Front: two Nanomanagements | 5 | No amount fixes the action shortage | One action short of installing and scoring Next Big Thing. |

**NE08 — Track the cheap Archives-Petty line as a high-priority reason to flip.** If the Corp begins on Gemilang with 3 credits and Petty Cash in Archives, a three-advancement score from HQ is feasible. A central success removes that specific simple line for next turn. It does not remove the Petty card or prevent a more expensive Nano combination.

**NE09 — Count operation combinations and first-action restrictions correctly.** Petty must be played before any action has finished; “I will click for money, then replay Petty” is not a valid line. Other income operations can change starting-credit thresholds if clicks permit. For example, front-side Archives Petty followed by Nano can score a three-advancement agenda from 5 starting credits; flipping therefore does not imply a universal 7-credit safety boundary.

Back-side Archives Petty followed by two Nanos can score Next Big Thing from 11 starting credits. Front-side three Nanos can supply six remaining actions for it from 17 credits. The simple table lists common routes, not an exhaustive combo solver.

**NE10 — Distinguish a Hub score from a two-pointer.** Superconducting Hub is a 3/1, not a 3/2; its scored ability improves Corp draw/maximum HQ size. It still turns money/click resources into an agenda point, and can be the last point. Do not use “three advancements” as shorthand for two points in the bot.

**NE11 — At Corp four or more points, prioritize preventing access to Next Big Thing.** It can finish from four, while two-pointers finish from five. At six, even Hub is enough. Use HQ pressure when a likely winning agenda is already retained; use R&D to intercept one before the draw. Identity denial matters most when it removes a feasible winning sequence, not simply because the back side is visible.

### HQ, R&D and remote priorities

**NE12 — Default to the cheaper productive central that also flips Gemilang.** Do not pay a large extra toll for R&D solely because “R&D pressure” is a slogan. Prefer HQ when you have evidence of retained agendas or scoring components; prefer fresh R&D when HQ is diluted and you can deny the next draw. Card knowledge overrides a fixed server preference.

**NE13 — Prefer fresh/multiple accesses to repeating known misses.** If R&D’s top card is known and unchanged, another run needs another benefit: flipping the currently back-side identity can supply that benefit once. Once front-side, repeated inspection of the same miss is normally inferior to draw/economy or another server.

**NE14 — Do not wait for a remote agenda to appear.** The deck can score without exposing one over your turn. Most persistent remotes are Regolith or Spin Doctor. Visit them to deny concrete value, not as your main agenda acquisition plan. If an agenda is actually left installed, however, steal it when affordable; the archetype label is not proof every remote is an asset.

**NE15 — Trash an exposed productive Regolith for 3 when it does not replace necessary central denial.** Denying several three-credit withdrawals can postpone Nano scores and expensive ICE rezzes. If only one withdrawal remains, a whole run plus 3-credit trash is usually poor unless it breaks a specific winning budget. When Gemilang can score next turn, a similarly priced central success often comes first.

**NE16 — Under Mahkota, price the asset at +2 for the whole run.** Regolith costs 5 to trash while that modifier applies; Spin Doctor costs 4. Trashing Mahkota for 2 does not remove the persistent modifier for the current run. If you access both, Grid plus Regolith costs 7 in trash payments, before ICE. Remove the Grid first when you want to eliminate future rez subsidies, not to claim an immediate same-run discount.

Its recurring credits can pay for ICE protecting that server or assets in its root, not operations. A Corp on zero pool credits may still rez a 2-cost Ping/Flyswatter there. It cannot use that same restricted pool to pay Nano or IP Enforcement.

### Tags: clear them for money and points

**NE17 — Default to ending untagged, with one click and 2 credits reserved per expected tag.** This list does not threaten a tag-based meat-damage operation, so one tag is not automatically lethal. It can still mean a five-credit transfer, resource destruction, stronger Predictive Planogram, or IP Enforcement. Clear before another optional setup action unless you are deliberately accepting those consequences for a better line.

**NE18 — IP Enforcement’s direct threshold equals the stolen agenda’s printed points.** The operation costs that many credits and removes that many tags. If any tags remain afterward, the reinstalled agenda gets one advancement. It goes into a server, not directly into the Corp’s score area; the Corp still has to score it.

| Your stolen agenda | Tags needed for direct repossession | Why one extra tag matters |
|---|---:|---|
| Superconducting Hub | 1 | At two tags, removing one leaves a tag and supplies one advancement. |
| Embedded Reporting / Tomorrow’s Headline | 2 | At three tags, the returned 3/2 starts at one advancement. |
| Next Big Thing | 3 | At four tags, the returned 5/3 starts at one advancement. |

**NE19 — Clear down below repossession thresholds when you cannot clear everything, but remember Bigger Picture.** One tag is still enough to take a stolen Hub. Two tags can take a stolen two-pointer. Bigger Picture can add another tag before IP, so a one-tag buffer below the direct threshold is not full protection. Zero tags shuts off both this tag-building use and direct repossession of every agenda in the list until another tag source occurs.

**NE20 — Repossession can become a same-turn score.** With three tags and a stolen 3/2, IP can remove two, spend 2 credits, and reinstall it with one advancement. Two manual advances cost 2 more credits and two actions. Even front-side Nebula can execute IP plus those two advances in its normal three-click turn. That is a two-point loss for you and a potential two-point gain for the Corp. Do not float three tags merely because there is no meat-damage operation.

**NE21 — Trash accessed IP for 5 when it is a real points threat, not automatically at every sighting.** Usually pay if you cannot reliably stay untagged and already hold a valuable target. If untagged with reliable cleanup, preserve money for central pressure when 5 would cripple access. There is only one copy, but Spin Doctor can recycle it; retain location knowledge. Bigger Picture has no printed trash cost, so normal access does not give you a basic paid-trash option for it.

**NE22 — Treat Bigger Picture as a transfer, not just a tax.** One tag can lose you up to 5 credits while giving the Corp the same amount. Clearing it for 2 and a click usually wins that trade, especially near an expensive scoring line. The transfer cannot exceed the money actually lost; do not give the Corp ten imaginary credits from a Runner who only had three.

### Grip and ICE decisions

**NE23 — Use route-specific grip targets, not a universal five-card rule.** From zero tags, one completely unbroken Doomscroll gives a tag and deals only one damage: after its first sub you have one tag, below the last sub’s threshold. With at least one tag on entering, all three subs produce three total damage. Two unbroken Doomscrolls from zero tags deal four total—one at the first, three at the second. Earlier Ping/Funhouse tags change the first calculation.

| Planned unprevented damage | Exact cards needed at damage windows | Working target |
|---|---:|---:|
| One Doomscroll, enter untagged, all subs resolve | 1 | 2–3 |
| One Doomscroll, enter with at least one tag, all subs resolve | 3 | 4 |
| Two Doomscrolls, start untagged, all subs resolve | 4 | 5 |

These targets do not pay for tag cleanup or protect randomly lost key cards. Playing a run event removes a grip card before encounters. No access ambush is waiting afterward in this snapshot, but Tomorrow’s Headline can add another tag on stealing.

**NE24 — Break Doomscroll’s tag sub when it prevents the extra two damage.** Starting with one tag, preventing the new tag keeps you at one and switches off its final conditional damage, saving both the new tag and two damage. Starting with two or more tags, breaking the tag sub alone does not switch that final damage off. Model the actual tag count between subs.

| ICE | Effect that matters | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Ping** — rez 2, strength 1 barrier | Tag on rez during the run; end-run sub. | Break the stop, then plan tag removal. Breaking does not prevent the rez tag. Subsequent runs through the already-rezzed card do not receive another rez tag. |
| **Funhouse** — rez 5, strength 4 code gate | At encounter, take a tag or the run ends. Its sub gives another tag unless you pay 4. | Count the compulsory-to-continue tag even with a decoder. Break the sub if efficient; paying 4 avoids only the additional tag. Each encounter repeats the first demand. |
| **N-Pot** — rez 4, strength 4 code gate | Can pay 3 per live end-run sub without a decoder; one/two/three relevant stops below threat 2 / at 2–3 / at 4+. | A 3-credit early native break can be good for access plus a flip. Repeated 9-credit native breaks are usually a sign to install the decoder or switch servers. |
| **Doomscroll** — rez 3, strength 3 sentry | Tag and conditional damage; no end-run sub. | A calculated early tank can secure a flip before a killer is ready, if grip and cleanup actually fit. Avoid turning one useful run into several turns of tag debt. |
| **Whitespace** — rez 2, strength 0 code gate | Lose 3 credits; end run if then at most 6 remain. | Need at least 10 before both unbroken subs to pass. Usually break; a loss of 3 followed by a bounce gives neither accesses nor a flip. |
| **Flyswatter** — rez 2, strength 0 code gate | Purge on rez, end-run sub. | Do not count virus counters that the rez will remove. |

**NE25 — Run early enough to clear the tags the route can actually produce.** One expected tag normally needs one later click and 2 credits; two need two clicks and 4. A first-click run through Funhouse may leave time to clear two tags, while a third-click run cannot do so with basic actions. If Headline is a possible non-winning steal, include its extra tag in the cleanup branch. Winning at seven or more points takes precedence over its pending on-steal tag.

### Worked choices

**Gemilang is showing, Corp has 3 credits and Petty Cash in Archives; HQ is cheap.** Prefer the HQ run now. Success removes the refund needed for the simple Archives-Petty three-advancement score. Do not report “Corp cannot score”: Nano plus other resources may still work.

**Identity already front-side; R&D top is a known operation.** Unless the run has another payoff, draw/economy or a different target normally beats repeating that access. No additional flip benefit exists.

**You have one tag and four cards; one Doomscroll ahead.** Breaking its tag sub can leave only its one-damage sub active. If you instead allow the tag, you rise to two and its final two damage becomes live. Spend a modest break cost to avoid the extra damage and cleanup.

**You stole an Embedded Reporting, have three tags, and Corp has 4 credits.** IP plus two advances can take it back and score it even on the front side. Clearing one tag removes the immediate one-advancement bonus, but direct repossession remains possible; clearing all three is the robust defense if affordable. A central run that flips Gemilang alone does not solve this front-side IP line.

**Regolith and Mahkota share a server.** A plan to trash Grid for 2 and Regolith for 3 during the same run is underbudgeted. The Regolith still costs 5 that run; both cost 7 total. Consider taking central value instead if this would exhaust access money.

**You are on four points and access Next Big Thing.** Steal it and win at seven. Do not reject the line over its pending Corp draw-counter benefit or hypothetical future IP punishment. Survive earlier ICE damage first.

### Mistakes to penalize in the Runner

| Severity | Observable mistake |
|---|---|
| Critical | Entering unavoidable Doomscroll damage with insufficient grip. |
| High | Leaving Gemilang’s visible cheap scoring line enabled while a useful affordable central success would disrupt it. |
| High | Assuming a flip disables all fast advance, ignoring front-side Nano/IP lines. |
| High | Floating enough tags for known affordable IP repossession when cleanup was available. |
| High | Treating an HQ Petty Cash as equivalent to an Archives replay in the Corp threat model. |
| High | Spending several turns waiting for a remote agenda instead of pressuring centrals. |
| Medium | Adding identity-denial value to every central run after the first flip. |
| Medium | Paying 9 repeatedly to native-break N-Pot when a cheaper sustainable route/tool exists. |
| Medium | Assuming a breaker cancels Ping’s rez tag or Funhouse’s encounter tag. |
| Medium | Underbudgeting Mahkota’s persistent asset tax or server-local rez credits. |
| Model error | Valuing Hub as two points, or giving the Runner its inactive Corp hand-size benefit. |

### Corp mistakes to punish

| Corp mistake | Runner exploitation |
|---|---|
| Leaves either HQ or R&D efficiently runnable while Gemilang is showing | Take access value and the flip before its next turn. |
| Spends on a remote while agendas accumulate in HQ | Attack HQ; this deck’s scoring pieces must pass through it. |
| Leaves the next R&D cards cheap before drawing them | Use fresh/multiple accesses to deny future scores. |
| Exposes a productive Regolith cheaply | Trash to interrupt expensive scoring/rez plans. |
| Builds central defense almost entirely around N-Pot before finding funds | Use its cheaper low-threat native payment for timely pressure. |
| Leaves a genuine agenda installed despite intending fast advance | Steal it if affordable; do not dismiss it as “must be economy.” |
| Reveals/searches a scoring operation with Embedded Reporting | Update the known R&D top and threat plan; avoid treating that top card as a fresh random agenda chance. |

### Policy state and research

Track identity side; whether a central success has already flipped it; first-operation refund state; Petty’s source and first-action restriction; Nano copies; Corp action/credit combinations; agenda values; your stolen agendas by printed value; tags at each ICE sub; accessible versus restricted credits; and known top-of-R&D information from Embedded Reporting. Its overadvancement counters search an operation onto R&D at discard end, not directly into HQ.

Do not import anti-fast-advance cards outside the actual Runner list. The base counterplay here is central access, identity disruption, money denial and tag control. Use a stronger denial plan only when the Runner actually has the necessary card/effect.
