# Core Battle Box: Runner matchup guides

## 02 — The Zwicky Group: Supermodernism Rush

**Default plan:** contest the first scoring remote before it becomes expensive, get a fracter online early, and protect your installed engine from Biawak. When Measured Response becomes live, finish successful-run turns with a deliberate survival budget. Do not let fear of the kill buy the Corp uncontested scores.

This guide targets the exact 49-card list in the [Card Gamer article’s published Zwicky image](https://cardgamer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Zwicky.png), inspected on 9 October 2026. The linked live NetrunnerDB page could not be fetched; subsequent revisions may differ. Numerical preferences are my proposed bot defaults, not tested win-rate estimates. Card arithmetic is exact under the stated assumptions. Assume ordinary hand size and no prevention unless the actual Runner changes them.

### Deck snapshot and its actual threats

| Type | Published list |
|---|---|
| Identity | The Zwicky Group: Invisible Hands |
| Agendas — 11 | 1 Above the Law; 3 Greenmail; 3 Off the Books; 3 Offworld Office; 1 Send a Message |
| Assets — 9 | 3 Anthill Excavation Contract; 3 Humanoid Resources; 3 Spin Doctor |
| Operations — 15 | 3 Government Subsidy; 3 Hedge Fund; 3 Key Performance Indicators; 3 Measured Response; 3 Petty Cash |
| ICE — 14 | 3 Brân 1.0; 3 Kessleroid; 1 Palisade; 2 Flyswatter; 2 Syailendra; 3 Biawak |

The identity draws a card the first time each turn an agenda or operation ability gains credits. Economy therefore also finds the next agenda, ICE, or kill card. The deck has three Responses, not PT Untaian’s two. It has no Byte!, Urtica, Phật, Clearinghouse, Orbital Superiority, or tag-producing card in this snapshot. Its only printed ICE damage is Syailendra’s one net damage. The large threats are **scoring speed, destruction of installed cards, and damage on the following Corp turn**.

| Card or package | Why fear it | Default response |
|---|---|---|
| **Measured Response ×3** | At threat 4+, after a successful run last turn, each copy costs 5 and offers 4 meat damage or an 8-credit payment. | Count one, two, and three-copy lines separately. Tags are irrelevant. Trash accessed copies for 3 when kill pressure is relevant. |
| **Biawak ×3** | Strength 6 sentry; rez 14, or 4 plus forfeiting an agenda. Can destroy a program and resource before ending the run. | Once a scored agenda and 4 credits exist, treat unknown ICE as a serious installed-card risk. Install a suitable killer or use a real bypass/protection plan. |
| **Offworld Office ×3** | A 4/2 score supplies 7 credits, potentially funding Responses immediately. | Contest the remote; never estimate next-turn kill money solely from the current pool. |
| **Off the Books ×3** | A 3/2 overadvanced agenda generates end-of-discard search counters. | Deny overadvanced scores when affordable; track the revealed searched card and update the kill/scoring plan. |
| **Greenmail ×3** | A 2/1 score gains 2 credits; forfeiting it gains 4. It supplies efficient Biawak fodder. | A cheap early steal denies money, a point, and a convenient forfeit. Do not price it as merely one point. |
| **Anthill Excavation Contract ×3** | Rez 3, trash 1; two start-turn payouts of 4 credits and a card. | Usually trash early, before the first payout, if access is cheap. |
| **Above the Law ×1** | Its 3/2 score can trash an installed resource without a tag. | Do not consider a vital resource safe merely because you are untagged. Denying this score can protect your draw/economy engine. |

The modern list has the classic rush/kill dilemma discussed in [The Satellite Uplink’s Supermodernism article](https://thesatelliteuplink.blogspot.com/2014/02/raging-bull-weyland-supermodernism-deck.html), but uses different cards and kill conditions. No SEA Source, Scorched Earth, or Snare! assumptions belong in this policy.

### Opening hand and first turns

**ZW01 — Prefer playable economy plus a cheap fracter or direct access to one.** Seven of the fourteen ICE are barriers. A fracter that handles two-subroutine Kessleroid is particularly useful. A killer/tutor is the next strong bonus, because a scored agenda turns Biawak on at only 4 credits.

Keep a strong economy-and-draw hand without a breaker if it can find tools quickly and exploit open servers. Mulligan a slow collection of expensive tools with no funding. Compared with the PT matchup, raise the value of a deployable fracter; compared with glacier, lower the value of a distant complete-rig payoff.

**ZW02 — Prefer one useful early run over an optional second setup install.** If an unprotected central or likely scoring remote is accessible and the Biawak check is clear, run before the Corp uses its next economy/draw turn to close it. Installing all three breaker types before interacting is normally too slow.

**ZW03 — Before the Corp scores an agenda, unknown ICE is less dangerous to your installed engine, not necessarily passable.** Without an agenda to forfeit, Biawak needs 14 credits. At lower pools, cheap barriers still stop runs and Syailendra can hurt. With no installed program/resource worth losing, the cost of a Biawak facecheck also changes—but do not assume the run will succeed.

**ZW04 — After Greenmail scores, switch the Biawak model immediately.** At 4 credits, the Corp can pay the reduced rez cost and forfeit Greenmail. Greenmail’s 4-credit refund occurs after paying the cost, so it cannot fund a rez from zero; it can restore the pool afterward. Forcing that rez may deny a point and consume an agenda, but need not drain money. The agenda’s credit ability can also trigger Zwicky’s draw on your turn.

### Run targets and timing

**ZW05 — Attack the scoring remote while it has one affordable layer.** Default to spending roughly 3–5 credits to steal an accessible two-pointer rather than allowing it while buying optional setup. The denied score often also denies money or a search engine. Spend more when it prevents a winning score or a decisive Offworld/Off the Books effect; spend less willingly when the apparent opening destroys your rig or leaves a Response kill.

**ZW06 — Run on click 1 or 2 when Brân or recovery matters.** Brân’s bioroid abilities consume clicks; Syailendra can remove a card. An early attempt leaves time to respond. Unlike PT Untaian, blind access itself has no listed damage ambush, so the timing concern is the route and the end-turn state rather than an automatic four-card access buffer.

**ZW07 — Check an exposed advanced remote without inventing an Urtica fork.** In this exact list the advanceable remote card is an agenda; there is no advanceable trap asset. Still account for unrezzed ICE and access restrictions from your own effects. If a card pool change adds traps, change this rule explicitly.

**ZW08 — Prefer fresh HQ/R&D accesses when the remote becomes disproportionately expensive.** Zwicky’s operation economy also draws, so central contents change frequently. Pressure after a money-and-draw turn; do not repeatedly inspect the same known R&D top card without a reward. HQ becomes especially valuable when observed draws exceed the Corp’s ability to install or score agendas.

**ZW09 — Trash early Anthill for 1 by default, and Humanoid Resources for 1 when cheap.** A fresh Anthill represents two payouts totaling 8 credits and two draws before emptying. Paying a click plus 1–3 total credits to remove it is usually excellent. After one payout, the remaining 4 credits and draw are still a useful denial target. Do not pay an enormous ICE toll solely because the trash cost is low.

Humanoid Resources spends three Corp clicks and itself to gain 4 credits, draw three, install up to two cards, and play an operation with its normal credit cost. Removing it cheaply prevents a compressed rebuilding turn. It can also play a single Response after gaining money; spending all three action clicks on the asset does not prohibit its embedded operation play.

**ZW10 — Trash accessed Response for 3 when threat is live or imminent, unless doing so creates the kill.** Removing a copy is real kill denial, but spending from 10 to 7 can eliminate the option to pay 8 against the remaining copies. Determine the post-trash survival line first. At low threat, preserving the first breaker or a scoring-window contest can take priority.

**ZW11 — Spin Doctor is secondary to immediate economy or scoring denial.** It can draw on rez and recycle before you arrive. Do not pay a large run toll expecting its 2-credit trash to prevent effects already taken. Track recycled Responses rather than counting discarded copies as permanently removed.

### What grip and credits to keep

**ZW12 — Before Response is available, do not compulsively refill to five before a damage-free route.** With no access traps and no damage ICE on the known route, playing useful cards down to a small grip can be correct. A known unbroken Syailendra damage sub needs one card to survive; prefer two if you will take that packet. Several encounters accumulate. Grip at the damage window, after playing a run event, is what counts.

**ZW13 — Once a Response line is plausible, plan the entire end-turn budget before running.** The condition is threat at least four—the higher score, not the sum—and a successful run on your preceding turn. A Corp score can raise threat before the operation is played. Successful Archives and asset runs qualify even without an agenda steal. Clearing tags does nothing to this condition.

| Affordable Response sequence, no other damage/draw/prevention | Exact minimum with ordinary grip sizes | Working target |
|---|---|---|
| One copy | 4 cards, or 8 spendable credits | 5 cards if taking the damage |
| Two copies | 4 cards + 8 credits; alternatively 16 credits | 5 cards + 8 credits |
| Three copies | 4 cards + 16 credits; alternatively 24 credits | 5 cards + 16 credits |

These credits must be usable for the out-of-run payment. Recurring breaker/run credits are not automatically eligible. Eight or twelve cards could absorb two or three copies, but ordinary end-turn hand-size limits prevent that plan without a specific modifier.

**ZW14 — Default to taking one isolated Response from five cards rather than paying 8.** It leaves one card, so surviving can be cheaper than paying. Override if another copy can follow, the random loss would ruin your only practical winning line, or preserved tempo is worth the money. Against two, pay one; against three, pay two. Do not greedily absorb the first hit without a plan for the rest.

**ZW15 — Do not reserve 16 credits from turn one.** Scale the reserve to feasible lines and information. Three copies require three plays and 15 total credits; two require two plays and 10. Start-turn Anthill income, fully advanced agenda scores, and operation sequencing can provide funds without consuming the needed Response actions. A hand known to contain a Response is stronger evidence than a copy somewhere unobserved in R&D.

Threat can fall as well as rise. If Biawak's forfeit drops the Corp below four points and you are also below four, Response is temporarily disabled; check whether a new score can restore it before the operation is played. Do not treat threat four as a permanent flag.

**ZW16 — Project score income explicitly.** Example: Corp on 2 points and 5 credits, with Offworld Office at three advancements. One advance costs a click and 1 credit; the score grants 7 credits, leaving 11 and two clicks at four points. Two Responses are now affordable. Your five-card, seven-credit ending position dies; five cards plus eight credits can pay one and survive the other.

A fully advanced Office can score without an advancement action, leaving all three actions for a triple-Response line if funds and cards suffice. Denying the agenda can therefore be the best kill defense.

**ZW17 — A no-successful-run recovery turn switches off Response, but may concede the rush.** Use one when drawing/money is necessary and the Corp cannot exploit it for a decisive score. Avoid alternating low-value successful runs with desperate recovery forever. Build enough capacity to contest the real remote while ending safely. If you already succeeded this turn, stopping further runs does not remove the existing flag.

### ICE decisions

| ICE | Relevant numbers/effect | Default break or encounter policy |
|---|---|---|
| **Kessleroid** | Rez 2, strength 1 barrier; two end-run subs; cannot be trashed by the Runner while rezzed. | Install a fracter early. Never select an ICE-trash line that this protection prohibits. |
| **Palisade** | Rez 3, strength 2, or 4 on a remote; one end-run sub. | Include the remote strength bonus. A central-server break quote understates the remote cost. |
| **Brân 1.0** | Rez 6, strength 6 bioroid barrier. Can install ICE immediately inward; two end-run subs. | Click through when it secures a timely score and leaves a safe ending state. Usually stop the install sub too when the Corp can add another unaffordable layer. |
| **Biawak** | Rez 14 or 4 plus an agenda; strength 6. Program destruction, resource destruction, then end-run. | Break destruction as well as the final stop. The Corp chooses its permitted effects and targets; you cannot volunteer a harmless program or assume it chooses to end the run instead. |
| **Syailendra** | Rez 4, strength 5 code gate. Can advance a card, make you lose 2 credits, and do 1 net damage. At three advancements, an encounter ability can add another advancement. | Break the advancement sub when it creates a scoring window; break the money/damage subs when they cross your Response thresholds. Breaking subs does not cancel the encounter advancement. |
| **Flyswatter** | Rez 2, strength 0 code gate; on-rez virus purge, then end-run sub. | Reprice the run after a purge. Breakers cannot cancel its on-rez ability. |

**ZW18 — Treat installed cards as assets at risk, not just sunk costs.** An unprotected killer install followed by a blind Biawak encounter can lose the killer before it achieves anything if it cannot actually break the sentry. Budget strength and all relevant subroutines. Losing your only fracter to Biawak often gives away the next two scoring windows.

**ZW19 — Do not pay to continue a run whose benefit has disappeared.** If Brân adds an unaffordable inner ICE, use the next legal jack-out opportunity. If damage or credit loss would make an otherwise successful run end in a Response kill, accepting an end-run can be better. This must be decided at legal windows, not after success is already recorded.

**ZW20 — Recalculate after Send a Message.** Its score or steal can rez an installed ICE for free. It can bring Biawak online without the agenda-forfeit payment, or fund a different large defender. The current Corp credit pool no longer bounds that rez. Do not decline a winning steal to fear a post-victory effect; for non-winning steals, preserve a viable next attack.

### Short worked decisions

**Cheap early remote; fracter ready; no agenda scored.** Prefer the contest over installing a decoder that does not help the route. The rush window is open now; a future ideal rig is not compensation for repeated scores.

**Greenmail scored; Corp has 4; only your fracter is installed.** A blind run can now lose that fracter to Biawak. Default: get the killer/protection first, or attack a known safe route. “Only four credits” is not a reason to ignore the sentry.

**Runner ends at five cards and eight credits; Corp has 15 and three available Responses.** This is not safe. Paying one leaves two four-damage packets, which exceed five cards. Need another 8 spendable credits, relevant prevention, a different previous run decision, or a win before the Corp turn.

**Response is live; an unprotected Anthill is available.** Trashing it may deny next-turn kill money, but its run also enables Response. Recompute the kill using the post-trash board. Do not mark economy denial automatically safe or automatically forbidden.

**Corp has a counter on Off the Books and searches a Response at discard end.** Record the revealed card as in HQ unless it is moved by a subsequent observed effect. Upgrade the threat estimate; do not keep using the generic three-copies-in-49 prior.

### Runner-specific knobs

| Runner | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| **Topan** | Use the discount to establish a fracter quickly, but count the installed card plus self-damage before a Response-enabled turn ends. Draw after setup; do not leave three cards because the install was efficient. |
| **MuslihaT** | Cheap pressure and forced rezzes suit the matchup. Transition out of casual successful runs when Response becomes live; preserve the out-of-run cash buffer even when run-only money makes access look cheap. |
| **Magdalene** | Accelerate the minimum usable rig. A large eventual economy advantage is useful only if the early remote is contested before the Corp reaches match point. Keep key resources protected from Biawak/Above the Law through score denial where possible. |

### Runner mistakes to penalize

| Severity | Observable decision error |
|---|---|
| Critical | Ending a successful-run turn dead to a known affordable Response line when a safe alternative existed. |
| Critical | Calling five cards plus eight credits safe against three Responses. |
| High | Ignoring Office income or Anthill payouts in the next-turn kill budget. |
| High | Blindly exposing an essential program/resource to affordable Biawak without a break/protection plan. |
| High | Repeatedly conceding affordable early remote contests while building optional rig pieces. |
| High | Spending below a needed 8/16-credit payment threshold to trash a noncritical asset. |
| Medium | Treating Greenmail’s forfeit as four credits of permanent Corp depletion. |
| Medium | Using a Runner trash effect against rezzed Kessleroid. |
| Medium | Allowing Syailendra’s credit/damage subs to cross an already calculated survival threshold. |
| Medium | Importing nonexistent access ambushes or tag-kill conditions from other Weyland/Jinteki lists. |

### Corp mistakes to punish

| Corp mistake | Default exploitation |
|---|---|
| Early remote protected only by a cheap barrier | Deploy the fracter and take the agenda before more layers arrive. |
| Anthill or Humanoid Resources left cheaply accessible | Trash for 1 before their compressed value is taken. |
| No scored agenda and too little money for full-cost Biawak | Use the lower installed-card risk to pressure open/cheap routes. |
| Too much protection moved to the remote | Take productive central accesses while preserving the Response ending budget. |
| Search reveals the next kill/scoring component | Use the new information to deny the enabling money, score, or successful-run condition. |
| Response money spent defending a low-priority attack | Recompute whether a higher-value follow-up now fits the survival budget. |
| A supposedly safe remote can be reached without losing your engine | Spend to deny Office/Off the Books rather than hoarding for an unnecessary perfect rig. |

### Policy implementation and sources

Track scored agendas available for forfeit; full and reduced Biawak rez affordability; Greenmail’s delayed refund; start-turn income; possible score income; Response locations and recursion; successful-run flag; both scores; post-run grip/cash; and the replacement cost of threatened installed cards. Separate “can break ICE” from “can access and survive the next Corp turn.”

The recommended action order is: win/stop the winning score; prevent a concrete kill; contest an affordable scoring window; remove cheap productive assets; take fresh central accesses; improve the next contest. Do not turn the threat of three hidden Responses into a permanent ban on running. Weight feasible hidden lines according to observed information.
